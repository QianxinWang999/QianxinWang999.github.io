Wu Lab @ Cuhk

My research aims to leverage innovative multi-source earth observations, modeling, and AI-driven analytical models, to explore the complex interplays between climate change, urban activities, and geohazard risks. Notably, the innovative AI-based earth observation method that I developed has been recognized as a valuable geospatial solution for sustainable development in the United Nations (UN) publication.

Research Areas

Subsidence Spatial Pattern Understading

We develop an oriented R-CNN deep learning network to automatically detect and classify subsidence bowls using InSAR measurements and multi-source ancillary data, including geological and lithological, land cover, topographic, and climatic data. We used 541 Sentinel-1 images acquired during 2015–2021 to map land subsidence of the Greater Bay Area by resolving persistent and distributed scatterers.

Deformation Time-Series Analysis

We propose a SAR-Transformer method to decompose InSAR time-series signals into various physics-related components and apply the method to evaluate the deformation of the world's longest cross-sea bridge, the Hong Kong-Zhuhai-Macao Bridge.

InSAR Atmospheric Delay Correction

We propose a bidirectional gated recurrent unit (BiGRU) model to correct random and seasonal atmopheric delays adaptively. The BiGRU model decomposes InSAR time-series measurements into non-seasonal and seasonal components by adopting a branched network structure and extracting component-wise features separately.

DEM Super-Resolution for Improving InSAR Topographic Phase Simulation

We constructed a real-world DEM super-resolution dataset, where the low-resolution and high-resolution DEMs were collected from SRTM and WorldDEM, respectively. An enhanced super-resolution generative adversarial network model was adapted to train on the dataset.

Our method is validated to improve the topographic phase simulation by 23.42% of MSE.

Nonstationary Effects of Natural and Human Factors on Land Subsidence

We apply spatial auto- and cross-correlation analysis to select the most reasonable explanatory variables from nine candidates in each city of the Greater Bay Area. City-level geographically weighted regression models were then constructed to explore the spatially nonstationary effects of natural and human-induced factors.

Natural and human-related factors accounted for 51.13% and 48.87%, respectively.
